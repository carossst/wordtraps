# Editorial Rules

These rules apply to quiz content, especially false-friend pairs, lexical traps, and explanation copy.

## Source Priority

1. Primary lexical meaning and standard usage
2. Reliable dictionary or grammar references
3. Secondary commentary or teaching materials

Secondary sources can help with framing, but they do not override the primary meaning distinction being tested.

## Core Principle

Content may be rewritten for readability, but not at the cost of precision.

Do not remove:
- a meaning distinction
- a usage boundary
- a register qualifier
- a context qualifier
- a positional or time qualifier
- a contrast that explains why the pair is a trap

If the distinction matters, keep it.

## Rewrite Standard

Allowed:
- shorter sentence structure
- cleaner word order
- less awkward phrasing
- more direct player-facing wording
- removal of redundancy that does not change meaning

Not allowed:
- replacing a precise meaning difference with a looser summary
- removing a qualifier that changes when a word is correct
- turning a lexical distinction into vague commentary
- using a friendlier wording if it weakens the contrast being taught

## Working Preference

Fast reading is good.

But precise contrast wins over punch.

The target is:
- quick to read
- easy to parse
- faithful to the actual distinction
- natural enough to sound like clear player-facing language

## Explanation Tone

Explanations should sound clearer and more direct than source material.

Target tone:
- direct
- plainspoken
- conversational without slang
- less stiff
- still exact on meaning and consequence

Allowed tone shifts in explanations:
- `does not` -> `doesn't`
- `is not` -> `isn't`
- `are not` -> `aren't`
- `X means` -> `Here, X means` when that helps parsing
- `may` -> `can` when this does not broaden the meaning

Preferred explanation style:
- say what each word means in practice
- surface the contrast fast
- favor the useful takeaway over abstract terminology
- keep the first line as the fastest useful explanation

Not allowed:
- adding jokes or hype
- replacing a precise term with a vague synonym
- simplifying away the actual contrast
- making the copy warmer if that changes the lexical meaning

## False-Item Standard

For items whose correct answer is `false`:
- prefer a clean false affirmative statement
- avoid wording that tests sentence parsing more than vocabulary knowledge
- avoid double negation
- avoid adding extra traps unrelated to the core lexical distinction
